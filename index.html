Independent Research

Two papers, one policy

Both use the NBA's 2023 "second apron" as a natural experiment — one at the player level, one at the team level — to ask how escalating regulatory costs change behavior.

How the NBA's Second Apron Reshapes Mid-Level Veteran Labor

Player fixed-effects models across three labor tiers show the apron didn't shrink the middle class of NBA rosters — it changed who occupies it.

  • −3.2 MPG Mid-level veterans lost 3.2 minutes per game post-apron, controlling for performance and team quality
  • χ², p=0.005 The mid-level salary band shifted meaningfully toward younger, cheaper players — confirmed against placebo cutoffs at 2017 and 2020
  • selection effect The players who remain in the band are measurably better: prime-age BPM improved from −0.56 to +0.43

How Escalating Regulatory Costs Shape Firm Capital Allocation

Using the NBA's luxury tax as a laboratory for corporate finance: when the cost of spending above a threshold keeps rising, firms don't just spend less — they concentrate capital at the top.

  • β=0.218, p<0.001 Teams closer to the tax line allocate significantly more payroll to their top three players the following season
  • one-year window Tax positioning is persistent for one season, then fades — spending resets faster than expected
  • robust Holds after ownership-transition exclusions, alternative lags, and reverse-causality checks
